BEFORE YOU BUY

Choose the shoe for your day

Choose the job before the look

Decide whether you mainly run, lift or dress casually. Start in that category, then compare cushioning, shape and materials. If two activities have very different demands, two appropriate pairs can be more useful than asking one shoe to do everything.

Let the fit rule a pair out

Try both shoes with your usual socks. Check toe room, heel security and pressure across the top of your foot. Do not rely on a universal rule to size up or down: follow the current model’s guidance and compare actual width options. Our shoe-fitting checklist gives you a practical starting point.

Compare the exact listing

Nike names can cover different generations, materials and colorways. Before ordering, confirm the version, size, seller, delivery details and return conditions. Compare the features you will use instead of assuming that a higher price means a better choice. Read how to judge a shoe’s value for more.

Your questions, answered

Which Nike shoe should I buy first?

Choose from your main activity. This draft starts with the Pegasus for daily running, the Metcon for strength-led training and the Air Force 1 for casual style. Compare the exact model and fit before making the final choice.

Are Nike shoes suitable for wide feet?

It depends on the model and the width offered in your market. Look for a documented wide option where available and try the actual pair. Do not assume that every model shares the same fit or that a longer size will solve a width problem.

Can I run regularly in Air Force 1s?

A dedicated running shoe is the better starting point for a regular running routine. In this shortlist, the Air Force 1 is a casual-style pick. Compare running-specific options for the distance, surface and fit you need.

Can one pair cover the gym and everyday wear?

Sometimes, if the pair meets the demands of your training and feels comfortable for your daily routine. Compare the Metcon and Free Metcon options for gym use, then assess their fit and feel yourself. Longer runs may justify a separate running shoe.
